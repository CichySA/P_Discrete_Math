\subsection{Implementation and Comparative Analysis of Simplex and Karmarkar Methods for Linear Programming}

\subsubsection{Definition and mathematical modeling}

Let's consider the general problem of optimization of finding the maximum or the minimum of a function.

Linear Programming consist in finding the best possible result in a mathematical model under of \emph{linear} constraints.

Let's define $x$ a vector that contains all decision's variables, $c$ the vector wih all the coefficients of the function, $A$ the matrix of constraints coefficients and $b$ the vector of limit of ressource.

The general problem is therefore 

\begin{equation}
    \inf_x c^T x \qquad \text{with}  \qquad \begin{cases}
        Ax\leq b\\
        x \geq 0 (?)
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

The space given by the constraints $Ax\leq b$ is therefore \emph{convex}.

\subsubsection{Solving the optimization problem}

The first way to solve it is to consider an important theorem of linear programming that tells that if a solution exist, it is situated on the border of the convex space.

Therefore, one of the first algorithm is the \emph{simplex} one that browse the border in order to find the solution.

The main problem of this algorithm is that it is not optimal to find the solution fast. Narendra Karmarkar proposed another algorithmme based on the idea of starting the search from the center of the space and not the border. This algorithm is iterative and, at each step, we reconsider the center point and go to the better point following the gradient way.

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Flow Network} -- A directed graph representing transport of flow between nodes.

\item \textbf{Edge Capacity} -- The maximum amount of flow that an edge can carry.

\item \textbf{Flow} -- Quantity transmitted through an edge, limited by its \textbf{capacity}.

\item \textbf{Node (Vertex)} -- A point in the network where flow can enter or leave.

\item \textbf{Arc (Edge)} -- A directed connection between two \textbf{nodes}.

\item \textbf{Flow Constraint} -- Flow on an edge cannot exceed its capacity.

\item \textbf{Flow Conservation} -- For intermediate nodes, incoming flow equals outgoing flow.

\item \textbf{Source} -- A node with only outgoing flow.

\item \textbf{Sink} -- A node with only incoming flow.

\end{itemize}
\subsubsection{Mathematical formulation of a flow network}
\[
G = (V,E)
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \(V\) is the set of vertices,
    \item \(E \subseteq V \times V\) is the set of directed edges,
    \item \(s \in V\) is the source,
    \item \(t \in V\) is the sink,
    \item \(c : E \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\) is the capacity function.
        \[
        \forall (u,v) \in E :
        \begin{cases}
        c(v,u) = 0, & \text{if } (v,u) \notin E
        \end{cases}
    \]
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Flow function}
\[
f : E \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}
\]
such that:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Capacity constraint}
    \[
    0 \le f(u,v) \le c(u,v)
    \qquad \forall (u,v)\in E
    \]

    \item \textbf{Flow conservation}
    \[
    \sum_{(u,v)\in E} f(u,v)
    \;=\;
    \sum_{(v,w)\in E} f(v,w)
    \qquad \forall v \in V \setminus \{s,t\}
    \]
\end{enumerate}
\paragraph{Excess Function} derived from \textbf{flow conservation}. It represents the difference between the total incoming and outgoing flow at vertex \(v\). For a flow network \(G = (V,E)\) with flow \(f\), the excess function of a vertex \(v\) is defined as

\[
e(v) =
\sum_{(u,v)\in E} f(u,v)
-
\sum_{(v,w)\in E} f(v,w)
\]



\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Conserving} vertex \(e(v) = 0\): Flow into the vertex equals flow leaving it.
\item \textbf{Active} vertex \(e(v) > 0\) indicating excess incoming flow. \textbf{Sink} \(t\) is active
\item \textbf{Deficient} vertex \(e(v) > 0\) indicating more outgoing than incoming flow. \textbf{Source} \(s\) is deficient


\end{itemize}
The value of a \textbf{feasible flow} (or just \textbf{flow}) for a network is the net flow into the sink \(t\):
\[
-e(s) = |f| = e(t)
\]

\subsubsection{Extensions of the problem}
\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Maximum Flow}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objective:} 
\[
\max \sum_{(s,v)\in E} f_{sv}
\]
\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Maximize total vaccines delivered from warehouses to hospitals subject to supply, transport and demand limits.
\item \textbf{Problem type:} Polynomial-time graph algorithm
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Minimum $s$-$t$ Cut}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objective:}
\[
\min \sum_{(i,j)\in C} c_{ij}
\]
\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Identify the smallest set of edges whose removal disconnects supply from hospitals (system bottlenecks).
\item \textbf{Problem type:} Max-flow Min-cut theorem, dual LP
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Lower-Bounded Flow}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objective:}
\[
\max \sum f_{sv}, \quad l_{ij} \le f_{ij} \le c_{ij}
\]
\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Guarantee each hospital receives a minimum allocation.
\item \textbf{Transformation:}
\[
f'_{ij} = f_{ij} - l_{ij}, \quad 0 \le f'_{ij} \le c_{ij} - l_{ij}
\]
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Weighted Flow Allocation}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objective:}
\[
\max \sum w_i x_i
\]
\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Prioritize certain hospitals (e.g., high-risk populations or larger hospitals).
\item \textbf{Problem type:} LP
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Max-Min Fairness}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objective:}
\[
\max z \quad \text{subject to } x_i \ge z
\]
\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Maximize the allocation of the worst-served hospital.
\item \textbf{Problem type:} LP
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Relative Coverage Fairness}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objective:}
\[
\max \alpha \quad \text{subject to } x_i \ge \alpha d_i
\]
\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Maximize the minimum percentage of demand satisfied across hospitals.
\item \textbf{Problem type:} LP
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Minimum-Cost Flow (MCFP)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objective:}
\[
\min \sum c_{ij} f_{ij}
\]
\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Minimize transportation costs or delays.
\item \textbf{Problem type:} LP
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Multi-Commodity Flow}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objective:}
\[
\max \sum_k \sum f_{ij}^k
\]
\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Allocate multiple resources simultaneously (vaccines, PPE, staff).
\item \textbf{Problem type:} Various formulations
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Integer Flow Constraints}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Constraint:}
\[
f_{ij} \in \mathbb{Z}
\]
\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Shipments occur in discrete batches.
\item \textbf{Problem type:} MILP, NP-hard
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Integer Capacity Expansion}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objective:}
\[
\max \sum_{(s,v)\in E} f_{sv}
\]
\[
0 \le f_e \le u_e + y_e
\]
\[
\sum_e y_e \le B, \quad y_e \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}
\]
\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Add (discrete) resources \(y_e\) (e.g., drivers) to increase capacity. 
\item \textbf{Problem type:} MILP, NP-hard if \(y_e \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\)
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Network Design / Planning}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objective:}
\[
\min \sum c_{ij} y_{ij}
\]
\[
\quad y_ij \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}
\]
\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Decide which transport routes should be built or expanded.
\item \textbf{Problem type:} MILP, NP-hard
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Proportional Fairness}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objective:}
\[
\max \sum_i \log(x_i)
\]
\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Balance efficiency and fairness by giving greater benefit to underserved hospitals.
\item \textbf{Problem type:} Convex
\end{itemize}


\item \textbf{Time-Expanded Network Flow}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Model:}
\[
G(t)=(V,E(t))
\]
\[
\max \sum_t \sum f_{sv}(t)
\]
\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Model distribution across multiple time periods with storage and delays.
\item \textbf{Problem type:} Dynamic graph problem
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Transforming Max Flow problem with constraints into Linear Program}
\paragraph{Standard Matrix Form of the Maximum Flow Problem}

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a directed graph with source \(s\), sink \(t\), and capacity vector
\[
u \in \mathbb{R}^m_{\ge 0},
\]
where the edges are indexed as
\[
E=\{e_1,e_2,\dots,e_m\}.
\]

Let the flow variables be collected in the vector
\[
x=\begin{bmatrix}
f(e_1)\\
f(e_2)\\
\vdots\\
f(e_m)
\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^m.
\]

Define the objective vector \(q \in \mathbb{R}^m\) by
\[
q_i =
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } e_i=(s,v)\in E, \\[4pt]
-1, & \text{if } e_i=(v,s)\in E, \\[4pt]
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
Then the value of the flow is
\[
|f| = q^T x.
\]
Which is equivalent to the familiar form we've presented earlier
\[
|f|
=
\sum_{(s,v)\in E} f(s,v)
-
\sum_{(v,s)\in E} f(v,s)
\]

Let \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{(|V|-2)\times |E|}\) be the node-edge incidence matrix for all intermediate vertices \(V\setminus\{s,t\}\), defined by
\[
A_{ve}=
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if edge } e \text{ enters vertex } v,\\
-1, & \text{if edge } e \text{ leaves vertex } v,\\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
Then the flow conservation constraints are
\[
Ax=0.
\]
Just to show an example, our first flow network can be represented as a product of \(Ax\), just as it can be stored in memory as a data structure (s and t rows can be reduced due to flow conservation - \(V\setminus\{s,t\}\)):
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
s \\
A \\
t
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{array}{c|ccccccccc}
 & e_{sa} & e_{sb} & e_{ab} & e_{ac} & e_{bd} & e_{cd} & e_{ct}& e_{dt}\\
\hline
s & -1 & -1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\\
a & 1 & 0 & -1 & -1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
b & 0 & 1 & 1 & 0 & -1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
c & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & -1 & -1 & 0\\
d & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 0 & -1 \\
t & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 \\
\end{array}
\]
\[
u=
\begin{bmatrix}
c_{sa} \\
c_{sb} \\
c_{ab} \\
c_{ac} \\
c_{bd} \\
c_{cd} \\
c_{ct} \\
c_{dt}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
5\\
2\\
2\\
3\\
4\\
1\\
2\\
5
\end{bmatrix}
\]
\[
q=
\begin{bmatrix}
q_{sa} \\
q_{sb} \\
q_{ab} \\
q_{ac} \\
q_{bd} \\
q_{cd} \\
q_{ct} \\
q_{dt}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
1\\
1\\
0\\
0\\
0\\
0\\
0\\
0
\end{bmatrix}
\]
\[
x=
\begin{bmatrix}
f_{sa} \\
f_{sb} \\
f_{ab} \\
f_{ac} \\
f_{bd} \\
f_{cd} \\
f_{ct} \\
f_{dt}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
3\\
2\\
1\\
2\\
3\\
1\\
1\\
4
\end{bmatrix}
\]

Therefore, the maximum flow problem in standard matrix form is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\max \quad & q^T x \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & Ax = 0, \\
& x \le u, \\
& x \ge 0.
\end{aligned}
\]
